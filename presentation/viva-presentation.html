Engine-level split prevents trajectory leakage
Why row-random splits leak:
In a turbofan trajectory, consecutive cycles share autocorrelated sensor readings. A row-random split places cycle t in training and cycle t+1 in test — the model effectively sees the answer. Engine-level splitting ensures no cycle from a test engine ever appears in training.
Fixed seed 42 · No rerolling · No stratification
Each of 100 training engines assigned to exactly one role

Conformal score: rank calculation and assumptions
Score computation:
For calibration engine i:
si = maxt∈trajectory |ŷ(i,t) − y(i,t)|

Sort 20 scores; finite-sample rank for 90% nominal coverage:
19th of 20 → q̂ = 107.8965 ≈ 107.90 cycles
Calibration covered 19/20 engines by construction — not validation
Key assumptions:
  • Exchangeability of calibration and future engines
  • Coverage is marginal, not conditional
  • Not guaranteed per-engine or per-trajectory
  • Calibration is separate from fitting
Why engine-maximum?
Row-level scores give valid row-marginal coverage but can miss the worst-case point in a trajectory. The engine-maximum ensures the interval would have covered every observed point for at least 90% of calibration engines.

The cost: the worst row drives q̂ for the entire engine, producing wide intervals (182.46 cycles mean width).
References: Vovk, Gammerman & Shafer (2005)
